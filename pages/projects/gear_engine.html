Solid Meshing-Gear Rotary Engine concept mockup for review · twin-half generator drive · muffler encasing · wound-field generator (no magnets) · brushless power transfer · efficiency optimiser

Two solid gears (laid out east–west, top-down view) mesh at the center and turn in opposite directions on their own shafts. Gas burns in the enlarged Chamber A on one side of the mesh and the expanding gas pushes on the angled gear teeth, spinning both gears apart. The deep tooth pockets carry leftover gas around the outside to the enlarged Chamber B, where the re-meshing teeth squeeze it (skip-fire compression). Intake and exhaust have no separate valves: a second valve gear on each shaft (one deck up, same size, locked in sync) uses its ported teeth to open and close the deck ports for both chambers. The “Fire every N revs” setting is the throttle. Purpose: drive a generator. v4 uses two engine halves flanking one generator: half A’s gears turn the rotor, the mirrored half B turns the stator the opposite way, and the two halves share a common combustion chamber/port so they fire together and stay in sync — efficiency is the goal.

Recommendation: (1) Benchmark with B now. A lean-burn gas CHP-grade genset (or a variable-speed diesel/inverter set) gives a proven 31–34% at 10–25 kW, or 35–40% at 50 kW and up, for the lowest $/kW. That is already 10–30% less fuel per kWh than 28%. (2) Use C for 24/7 baseload if the budget allows: 55% cuts fuel per kWh by ≈49% vs 28%. (3) Gear engine: build a dyno prototype first, and only add the muffler-wall TEG or an exhaust turbine once it measures ≥28%. (4) If you want to invent: move R&D toward D, the free-piston linear generator. It meets all four goals (efficiency, low parts, long life, counter-moving balance) better than the gear engine.

Microturbines were checked and dropped: they have one moving part on air bearings and 60k–80k h life, but only 26–29% at 30–65 kW (Capstone C65 28–29%), so they don’t beat the target. Fuel per kWh electric: 28% → 3.6 kWh · 33% → 3.0 · 40% → 2.5 · 55% → 1.8 (est.).

Valve deck — gear-tooth porting

  • No gate valves. Each shaft carries a 2nd gear — a valve gear (VG1, VG2) one deck above its power gear: same size, same teeth, same rpm, keyed to the shaft so it can never drift out of sync.
  • VG1 and VG2 mesh with each other like the power gears, sealing the deck midline.
  • Most tooth gaps are blind (closed floor = valve shut). A short sector of through-cut “ported” teeth lets gas through when it sweeps a port in the deck plate.
  • VG1 ported teeth → intake port into Chamber A (0°–40°).
    VG2 ported teeth → exhaust port out of Chamber B (200°–330°).
  • Re-timing = re-index the valve gear on its shaft spline. No cams, springs, or extra shafts.
  • Why stacked on the shaft: a separate 1:1 valve gear meshing alongside would have to be the same size as the power gear and would collide with the chambers; coaxial stacking gives exact sync with zero added backlash.

Port timing (per rev of Gear 1)

  • 0°–40° intake open (Chamber A) — VG1 ported teeth over port
  • 45° fire (on fire revs only)
  • 45°–200° expansion on teeth
  • 200°–330° exhaust open (Chamber B) — VG2 ported teeth over port

Design notes (v2)

  • Labyrinth teeth: angled faces + deep pockets + tip groove make a winding leak path. Blow-by is reduced, not eliminated — a little leakage is intentional (no rubbing seals, no lubrication-starved contact).
  • Hot-running clearance: housing bore is sized so the gap is correct at operating temperature after thermal growth. Cold, it is deliberately looser.
  • Solid gears, no hollow rotors, no gate or poppet valves: 4 gears on 2 shafts (2 power gears + 2 valve gears) — still only 2 rotating assemblies.
  • Skip-fire: non-firing revs re-compress leftover gas in Chamber B.
  • Efficiency trade (v3): blow-by past the labyrinth teeth and the hot-running clearance cost some efficiency (see the orange row in “Where the fuel energy goes”) — but they simplify sealing enormously: no apex/face seals, no seal lubrication, no seal wear.
  • Generator drive: both shafts turn at identical rpm in opposite directions (locked by the mesh), so they can drive the generator directly — no gearbox.
  • v4 twin halves: a second, mirrored gear pair (half B) sits on the far side of the generator. Half A’s CCW gear is coaxial with the rotor; half B’s CW gear is coaxial with the stator — so the counter-rotation comes straight from the gears, no belt.
  • v6 wound-field generator (no magnets): rotor = DC field winding (copper or aluminium wire on laminated iron poles); stator = 3-phase armature winding. Because both parts spin, slip rings carry DC field current in and AC power out. Output voltage is set by the field current (built-in regulation; field off = generator off, even while spinning). Costs ≈ 1–3% excitation power (est.).
  • Winding thermal limits: class F insulation ≈ 155 °C, class H ≈ 180 °C hot-spot; copper resistance rises ≈ 0.4%/°C (more loss when hot). Aluminium wire is lighter/cheaper but has ≈ 61% of copper’s conductivity → needs ≈ 1.6× the cross-section or runs hotter. Inside the hot muffler jacket, keep hot-spot below the insulation class (insulated inner wall and/or cooling air).
  • v8 efficiency optimiser: sweeps every lever through the rough model; biggest wins are residual-gas squeeze and matching a bigger chamber with a longer fire-every N (let the gas fully expand). Brushless costs a little efficiency vs slip rings but is kept for lifetime. Estimates.
  • v7 brushless transfer (long uptime): preferred = brushless exciter (field in) + rotary transformer with a spinning HF converter (power out). Capacitive coupler and slip rings shown for comparison; see the options panel under the side view. Figures are estimates.
  • v5 muffler encasing: the whole engine (both halves + generator) sits inside a permanent hollow double wall. Exhaust ports dump straight into it; perforated baffles divide the cavity into chambers so the pulses lose energy before the single tailpipe. Cooling fins cover the entire outer skin. Replaces a separate muffler + exhaust pipework.
  • Shared chamber / port: both halves burn in one common chamber (crossover port between them). One ignition pressurises both halves at the same instant → equal push, same firing timing → the halves stay in step.

Model assumptions (all rough)
  • Gear pair ~200 mm tip dia, 50 mm face → tooth-pocket displacement ≈ 0.8 L/rev (both gears).
  • Firing rev expands gas over 45°→200° (43% of that); each coast rev adds ~50% more expansion volume (rest vented/leaked).
  • Charge = chamber volume × compression ratio of fresh mixture at 85% fill, 95% burned; constant-volume burn; polytropic expansion (γ ≈ 1.30–1.34). Net work counted against 1 atm on the back of the teeth, minus compression work.
  • “Coast revs needed” = revs until gas drops to ~1.3 bar abs (little useful push left below that).
  • Wall heat + finite burn keep ~68–80% of ideal work (bigger chamber = less surface per volume = better).
  • Blow-by ≈ 5% of charge at hot clearance; ×2.4 cold; ×1.6 hydrogen; rises at low rpm and with long coast holds.
  • Friction: bearings + mesh ≈ 0.35 N·m + windage (no rubbing seals). Generator = resistive load sized for ≈3,000 rpm at the default settings; 9,000 rpm cap.
  • Twin-half mode (v4): each half modelled as one copy of this engine (same chamber size per half, shared chamber = both halves’ volume) → power & fuel ×2, efficiencies unchanged except coupling (≈99%, slip rings + bearings, no belt) and generator at 2× relative speed. Sync loss from the gas-only link is not modelled.
  • Generator (all modes, v6): wound-field, no permanent magnets → generator η lowered ≈ 2 points vs PM for field excitation (est.). Winding temperature effects not modelled.
  • Rotating power transfer (v7): output path ≈ 97% brushless exciter + rotary transformer, ≈ 93% capacitive, ≈ 99% slip rings (applied to modes where the stator spins); stationary-stator modes only see the field-feed factor. All est.

Generator coupling

Layout. Engine half A’s CCW gear drives the wound field rotor (electromagnet, DC via slip rings) directly; the mirrored half B’s CW gear drives the wound armature stator shell the opposite way. Relative speed = 2× shaft rpm with no belt and no gearbox. The halves share one combustion chamber / crossover port, so each ignition pushes both halves at the same instant → equal torque, firing timing kept in sync. Costs an extra bearing set (≈ 0.5%, est.) plus the field-in / power-out transfer chosen below (brushless preferred); wound-field excitation ≈ 1–3% (in generator η, est.). No permanent magnets.
