VectorMorph

A sampling engine that makes a multisampled instrument respond continuously instead of in steps. It powers my own instruments, and it is available to license for yours.

The Problem It Solves

A real instrument does not simply get louder when you play it harder. It changes character — the stick bites, the head speaks differently, the cymbal opens up. Sample libraries capture this by recording the instrument at several dynamic levels, and I have spent decades recording those layers.

Every sampler then does the same disappointing thing with them: it picks one, or crossfades two. Crossfading does not produce the tone in between. It produces two different recordings playing at once, which is a different sound entirely, and the seam is audible. Play a sustained roll and you also hear the other half of the problem — the same handful of samples cycling, the machine-gun effect that every drum programmer knows and works around.

What It Delivers

VectorMorph does not crossfade, and it does not synthesize. Where a real recording exists, you hear that recording, untouched. Everywhere in between, the engine produces the tone that genuinely belongs at that point — derived from the real recordings around it rather than mixed from two of them.

The effect on a player is immediate. The instrument stops behaving like a set of samples with boundaries and starts behaving like an instrument.

  • Continuous responseVelocity, strike zone, and hi-hat openness are physically continuous on a real instrument, so they are continuous here rather than stepped into layers
  • No audible layer seamsNo crossfade artifacts and no pitch seam where one layer hands over to the next
  • No machine-gun effectRepeated notes vary inherently rather than cycling a fixed set of round-robins, so sustained rolls and fast passages hold up
  • Real recordings throughoutNothing is synthesized from parameters, nothing is neural, and nothing is resampled from a second room. Every operand is a real take
  • Works with the library you already haveThe engine is built to sit over existing multisampled material rather than requiring the instrument be recorded again
  • Validated by earEvery engine was signed off by listening to it, on real instruments, before it was called done

Where the Name Comes From

The "vector" is the position in the morph space, and the idea descends from the vector synthesis of the Sequential Prophet VS and the Korg Wavestation — travelling between corner sounds by moving a point around. Those machines did it with oscillators. VectorMorph applies the same instinct to real recorded material, which is a harder problem and a more useful one.

The first instrument built on it is the Larry Seyer Acoustic Drums library, the same library I have been recording and programming since the Gigasampler days.

Proven In

VectorMorph is a self-contained engine with no dependency on any one product, which is what makes it portable to someone else's instrument. It runs in:

  • OTTOUses it to play its drum library with continuous velocity and zone response
  • VectorSamplerThe instrument built around it, generalizing it past drums
  • IDAInherits it by embedding OTTO complete

The LOUIS effects plugins share the same design philosophy — travel continuously through a space rather than pick from a menu of presets — but they are effects, and they reach it by their own means rather than by running this engine.

Who It Is For

The problem VectorMorph solves is not specific to drums, or to my libraries. It belongs to anyone whose instrument is built from real recordings at discrete levels — which is very nearly everyone who samples acoustic instruments:

  • Instrument and plugin developersGive an existing sampler engine continuous response without re-recording the source material
  • Sample library developersLet a library play the way it was heard in the room, rather than the way the layer boundaries allow
  • Game and interactive audioContinuous dynamic response matters more where performance is generated rather than performed, and where repetition is constant
  • Instrument manufacturersElectronic and hybrid instruments whose expressiveness is limited by the playback engine rather than by the controller
